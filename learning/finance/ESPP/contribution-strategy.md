# ESPP Contribution Strategy

> There is no universally correct contribution percentage. The appropriate contribution depends upon your cash flow, financial goals, risk tolerance, and overall investment strategy.

---

## How Should I Decide?

Never decide based upon:

```text
Company's stock price is increasing.

Therefore,

I should select 15%.

= WRONG APPROACH
```

Always decide based upon:

```text
Salary
   -
Taxes
   -
Monthly Expenses
   -
EMIs
   -
SIPs / Investments
   -
Emergency Fund Allocation
   -
Other Financial Commitments
   =
Available Monthly Amount
```

Ask yourself:

```text
Can I comfortably afford the deduction
for the entire offering period?

YES
↓

Proceed.

NO
↓

Reduce contribution percentage.
```

---

## Contribution Decision Framework

Before selecting the contribution percentage, ask:

- Do I have an emergency fund?
- Can I comfortably afford the deduction?
- Are my monthly expenses manageable?
- Do I have significant debt obligations?
- Am I comfortable with stock market risks?
- Is my portfolio adequately diversified?
- Do I understand my company's ESPP policies?
- Do I have a hold or sell strategy?

---

## Choosing the Right Contribution Percentage

### 2%

Best For:
- New employees.
- Beginners.
- Conservative investors.
- Employees evaluating their company's ESPP.

---

### 5%

Best For:
- Comfortable cash flow.
- Conservative approach.
- Moderate participation.
- Employees starting their ESPP journey.

---

### 10%

Best For:
- Balanced approach.
- Good cash flow.
- Comfortable with investment risks.
- Moderate-to-high participation.

---

### 15%

Best For:
- Excellent cash flow.
- Adequate emergency savings.
- No significant debt obligations.
- Comfortable with reduced take-home salary.
- Employees who fully understand their ESPP benefits and risks.

---

## When Should I Choose Maximum Contribution?

Maximum contribution may be appropriate when:

- Emergency fund is available.
- Monthly cash flow remains comfortable.
- No major expenses are approaching.
- Contribution deductions do not create financial stress.
- Portfolio diversification is maintained.
- You understand taxation implications.
- You understand your company's ESPP policies.

---

## When Should I Avoid Maximum Contribution?

Avoid maximum contribution if:

- Living paycheck-to-paycheck.
- Significant debt exists.
- No emergency fund is available.
- Major expenses are approaching.
- Monthly cash flow becomes uncomfortable.
- You do not understand the associated risks.
- Your portfolio is already heavily concentrated in employer stock.

## Contribution Percentage Selection Guide

| Situation | Suggested Contribution |
|-----------|----------------------|
| Beginner | 2%-5% |
| Conservative | 5% |
| Balanced | 10% |
| Excellent Cash Flow | 15% |
| No Emergency Fund | Avoid Maximum Contribution |
| Significant Debt | Lower Contribution |
| Financial Stress | Lower Contribution |
| Strong Cash Flow + Good Risk Management | Higher Contribution |

## Hold or Sell Strategy

Before enrollment, decide whether you intend to:

```text
Purchase Shares
       |
 -----------------------
 |                     |
Hold                  Sell
 |                     |
Long Term            Immediate
Investing            Diversification
 |                     |
Portfolio            Wealth Creation
Allocation
```

There is no universally correct strategy.

Possible approaches include:
- Sell all.
- Hold all.
- Partial sell and partial hold.

The appropriate strategy depends upon:
- Financial goals.
- Risk tolerance.
- Portfolio allocation.
- Employer concentration risk.

---

## Common Mistakes

Avoid:

- Selecting the maximum percentage because colleagues are doing so.
- Selecting the maximum percentage because the stock recently increased.
- Ignoring monthly cash flow requirements.
- Ignoring concentration risk.
- Ignoring taxation implications.
- Ignoring portfolio diversification.

---

## Checklist Before Enrollment

- Comfortable cash flow?
- Emergency fund available?
- Major expenses planned?
- High-interest debt absent?
- Portfolio diversified?
- Understand company policies?
- Understand taxation implications?
- Comfortable with investment risks?
- Hold or sell strategy decided?

---

## Key Takeaways

- Higher contribution percentages are not always better.
- Never compromise your emergency fund or monthly cash flow.
- Understand both the risks and benefits before selecting the contribution percentage.
- Treat ESPP as part of an overall investment strategy rather than an isolated investment decision.

> ### Rule of Thumb
>
> Select the highest contribution percentage that you can comfortably maintain throughout the offering period without compromising your financial stability or portfolio diversification.
