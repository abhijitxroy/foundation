# ESPP Basics

> Employee Stock Purchase Plan (ESPP) allows eligible employees to purchase their employer's stock at a discounted price through payroll deductions.

---

## What is ESPP?

ESPP is one of the most valuable employee benefits offered by some companies.

Employees contribute a percentage of their salary during an offering period and purchase company shares at a discounted price on the purchase date.

Some plans may also provide:
- Look-back feature.
- Immediate sell options.
- Contribution refunds.
- Automatic enrollment continuation.

---

## Who is Eligible?

Eligibility depends upon company policies.

Common requirements include:

- Full-time employees.
- Permanent employees.
- Employees in participating countries.
- Employees meeting payroll requirements.

Some companies may exclude:
- Contractors.
- Interns.
- Temporary employees.
- Employees from non-participating locations.

Always verify:
- Eligibility requirements.
- Enrollment periods.
- Contribution limits.
- Purchase dates.
- Tax implications.

---

## Important Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|------|--------|
| Enrollment Period | Period during which employees can enroll or increase contributions. |
| Offering Period | Period during which payroll deductions are accumulated. |
| Purchase Date | Date on which shares are purchased. |
| Contribution Percentage | Percentage deducted from salary. |
| Discount | Discount provided on the purchase price. |
| Look-back Feature | Lower of offering date price or purchase date price is used (if supported). |
| Payroll Deduction | Amount deducted from salary towards ESPP. |
| Contribution Limit | Maximum contribution percentage allowed by the company. |
| Subscription Period | Duration during which contributions are accumulated. |
| QUICKSale | Automatic sale feature provided by some companies. |

## Advantages

- Purchase shares at a discount.
- Potential wealth creation opportunity.
- Payroll deductions make investing systematic.
- Some plans provide Look-back benefits.
- Some plans allow immediate selling of purchased shares.
- Contribution refunds may be available in certain situations.

---

## Risks

ESPP is NOT risk free.

Potential risks include:
- Stock price volatility.
- Reduced monthly cash flow.
- Concentration risk.
- Tax implications.
- Employer-specific risks.
- Portfolio imbalance.

## When Should I Enroll?

ESPP may be suitable when:

- Cash flow is comfortable.
- Emergency fund is available.
- High-interest debt is absent.
- You understand the risks involved.
- Portfolio diversification is maintained.
- You understand the taxation implications.

---

## When Should I NOT Enroll?

Reconsider participation if:

- Living paycheck-to-paycheck.
- No emergency fund exists.
- Significant debt obligations exist.
- Major expenses are approaching.
- Monthly cash flow is already constrained.
- You do not understand the company's ESPP policies.

---

## Good ESPP vs Poor ESPP

| Parameter | Good | Better | Excellent |
|----------|------|--------|-----------|
| Discount | 5% | 10% | 15%+ |
| Look-back | No | Optional | Yes |
| Contribution Limit | 5% | 10% | 15%+ |
| Immediate Sell | No | Optional | Yes |
| Contribution Refund | No | Optional | Yes |

---

## Key Takeaways

- Understand the company's ESPP policies before enrolling.
- Higher contribution percentages are not always better.
- Never ignore concentration risk.
- Maintain adequate emergency savings.
- Look-back features significantly improve ESPP benefits.
- ESPP should complement an overall investment strategy.

> ### Rule of Thumb
>
> Participate in ESPP only when it aligns with your cash flow, financial goals, and overall portfolio allocation.
