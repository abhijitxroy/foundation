# Vesting, Taxation and Selling

> Receiving vested shares is not the end of the RSU journey. Employees should understand taxation, concentration risk, and how RSUs fit into their overall investment strategy.

---

## How Does Vesting Work?

```text
          Grant Date
               |
             RSUs
               |
        Vesting Schedule
               |
        ---------------------
        |         |         |
      Year-1    Year-2    Year-3
        |         |         |
      Vest       Vest      Vest
        |         |         |
   Shares Received Over Time
```

Vesting schedules vary across companies and may include:
- Annual vesting.
- Quarterly vesting.
- Monthly vesting.
- Cliff vesting.
- Company-specific vesting schedules.

Always understand:
- Grant date.
- Vesting dates.
- Vesting percentages.
- Refreshers.
- Promotion grants.

---

## Taxation Basics

Employees should understand:

- When taxation applies.
- How vested shares are taxed.
- Capital gains taxation.
- Country-specific taxation requirements.
- Employer withholding policies.
- Annual tax filing requirements.

Always verify:
- Payslips.
- Tax statements.
- Brokerage statements.
- Country-specific tax regulations.

> Tax laws differ across countries and may change over time. Always refer to applicable regulations when planning taxes.

---

## What Happens After Vesting?

```text
          Shares Vest
                |
            Taxation
                |
        -------------------------
        |            |            |
      Hold         Sell      Partial Sell
        |            |            |
    Long Term      Diversify    Balanced
    Investing                    Approach
```

Employees generally have three choices:
- Hold all shares.
- Sell all shares.
- Partially sell and partially hold.

There is no universally correct approach.

---

## Should I Hold?

Holding may be appropriate when:

- Long-term conviction exists.
- Portfolio allocation supports it.
- Concentration risk remains acceptable.
- Financial goals align with long-term investing.

Always ask:

```text
Can my portfolio comfortably
handle additional exposure to
my employer's stock?

↓

YES

↓

Holding may be reasonable.


NO

↓

Consider diversification.
```

---

## Should I Sell?

Selling may be appropriate when:

- Portfolio diversification is required.
- Concentration risk is increasing.
- Financial goals require liquidity.
- Rebalancing is necessary.

Selling vested shares does NOT imply:
> Lack of confidence in the company.

Diversification remains an important investing principle.

---

## Should I Partially Sell?

A balanced approach may include:

```text
100 Shares Vest

       |
-----------------------
|                     |
Sell 50             Hold 50
|                     |
Diversify          Long Term
                   Investing
```

Partial selling allows employees to:
- Diversify investments.
- Retain long-term exposure.
- Manage concentration risk.
- Rebalance portfolios periodically.

---

## Concentration Risk

Avoid:

```text
Salary
   +
RSUs
   +
ESPP
   +
Future RSUs
   +
No Diversification

↓

Everything depends on
one company.
```


Prefer:

```text
Salary
   +
RSUs
   +
Diversified Portfolio

↓

Index Funds
+
Flexicap Funds
+
Debt Allocation
+
Emergency Fund
+
Other Investments
```

---

## RSU + ESPP Strategy

Always evaluate:

```text
Salary
   +
Bonuses
   +
RSUs
   +
ESPP
   +
Future Stock Grants
```

Ask yourself:

- What percentage of my portfolio depends upon my employer?
- Am I adequately diversified?
- Am I comfortable with the associated risks?

Never ignore:
> Employer concentration risk.

---

## Common Mistakes

Avoid:

- Ignoring taxation implications.
- Holding every vested share indefinitely.
- Ignoring portfolio diversification.
- Making investment decisions based solely on recent stock price movements.
- Ignoring concentration risk.
- Failing to periodically review portfolio allocation.

---

## Portfolio Allocation Checklist

Before deciding whether to hold or sell, ask:

- Am I adequately diversified?
- Do I understand the taxation implications?
- Am I comfortable with employer concentration risk?
- Does holding these shares align with my financial goals?
- Is portfolio rebalancing required?

---

## Key Takeaways

- Vesting schedules differ across companies.
- Understand taxation before making investment decisions.
- Hold, sell, and partial sell are all valid strategies.
- Portfolio diversification remains important.
- Never ignore employer concentration risk.
- Review investment decisions periodically as financial goals evolve.

> ### Rule of Thumb
>
> Treat vested RSUs as part of your overall investment portfolio rather than as separate assets. Every decision to hold employer stock should be intentional and consistent with your long-term financial goals and risk tolerance.
