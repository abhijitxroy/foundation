# RSU Basics

> Restricted Stock Units (RSUs) are company-provided stock grants that form an important part of employee compensation and long-term wealth creation.

---

## What are RSUs?

RSUs are company-provided stock grants that are awarded to employees and become available according to a vesting schedule.

Companies may provide RSUs as:
- Joining grants.
- Annual refreshers.
- Promotion grants.
- Performance-based compensation.
- Retention incentives.

Unlike ESPP, employees do not purchase RSUs. They are awarded by the employer as part of the compensation package.

## Why Do Companies Provide RSUs?

Companies typically provide RSUs to:

- Attract talent.
- Reward performance.
- Improve employee retention.
- Align employee and shareholder interests.
- Encourage long-term contributions.

---

## Types of RSU Grants

Employees may receive:

- Joining Grants.
- Annual Refreshers.
- Promotion Grants.
- Retention Grants.
- Special Recognition Grants.

Always understand:
- Grant amount.
- Vesting schedule.
- Compensation cycle.
- Promotion policies.
- Tax implications.

---

## Important Terminologies

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Grant Date | Date on which RSUs are awarded. |
| Vesting Date | Date on which shares become available to the employee. |
| Vesting Schedule | Timeline determining when shares vest. |
| Joining Grant | RSUs provided while joining a company. |
| Annual Refresher | Additional RSUs granted periodically based on company policies and performance. |
| Promotion Grant | Additional RSUs granted upon promotion. |
| Unvested Shares | Shares that have not yet vested. |
| Vested Shares | Shares that belong to the employee after vesting requirements are met. |

## Advantages of RSUs

- Valuable component of employee compensation.
- Potential long-term wealth creation.
- No upfront investment required.
- Additional grants may be available during employment.
- Aligns employee contributions with company success.

---

## Risks of RSUs

RSUs are NOT risk free.

Potential risks include:
- Stock price volatility.
- Concentration risk.
- Tax implications.
- Losing unvested shares upon leaving the company.
- Reduced diversification if employer stock dominates the portfolio.

## Common Employee Mistakes

Avoid:

- Negotiating only base salary.
- Ignoring annual refreshers.
- Ignoring vesting schedules.
- Ignoring compensation cycles.
- Holding every vested share indefinitely.
- Ignoring taxation implications.
- Ignoring concentration risk.

---

## Questions Every Employee Should Ask

Before joining:

- Are RSUs included in the compensation package?
- What is the vesting schedule?
- Are annual refreshers provided?
- Are promotion grants available?
- What is the compensation review cycle?
- What are the taxation implications?

After joining:

- What are the performance expectations?
- How are refreshers determined?
- How are promotion decisions made?
- What additional impact is expected at my level?

## Who is Eligible?

RSU eligibility varies across companies and may depend upon:

- Job level or grade.
- Role and responsibilities.
- Compensation policies.
- Location and country.
- Business organizations.
- Hiring level.
- Promotions.
- Performance.
- Annual compensation decisions.

Some companies may provide:
- Joining grants.
- Annual refreshers.
- Promotion grants.
- Retention grants.

Others may provide RSUs only from specific job levels or grades.

Always verify:

- Eligibility requirements.
- Compensation policies.
- Vesting schedules.
- Promotion policies.
- Compensation review cycles.

> Never assume that all employees within a company are eligible for RSUs or receive identical stock grants.

---

## Key Takeaways

- RSUs are an important component of total compensation.
- Understand both the grant amount and the vesting schedule.
- Refreshers and promotion grants can significantly impact long-term wealth creation.
- Never ignore taxation and concentration risks.
- Compensation discussions should begin well before compensation decisions are finalized.

> ### Rule of Thumb
>
> Treat RSUs as a combination of compensation, career growth, and wealth creation. Evaluate them alongside salary, bonuses, benefits, and future growth opportunities rather than in isolation.
