# Requirements Engineering

## Overview

Requirements Engineering is the discipline of discovering, analyzing, documenting, validating, and managing what a software system must accomplish. Poor requirements are one of the leading causes of project failure, rework, delays, and cost overruns. Strong engineering begins with understanding the problem before designing the solution.

## Definition

Requirements Engineering is the systematic process of identifying stakeholder needs and translating them into clear, testable, and maintainable software requirements.

## Objectives

- Understand business problems before proposing solutions.
- Align stakeholders around a common vision.
- Reduce ambiguity and unnecessary rework.
- Establish measurable acceptance criteria.
- Build software that delivers business value.

## Types of Requirements

| Type | Purpose |
|------|---------|
| Business Requirements | Define business goals and expected outcomes. |
| Functional Requirements | Describe what the system must do. |
| Non-Functional Requirements | Define quality attributes such as performance, scalability, availability, and security. |
| Technical Constraints | Capture technology, compliance, and operational limitations. |

## Best Practices

- Ask "why" before "how".
- Validate assumptions early.
- Keep requirements testable and measurable.
- Prioritize based on business value.
- Treat requirements as living artifacts.

## Common Mistakes

- Building features without validating the problem.
- Confusing solutions with requirements.
- Ignoring non-functional requirements.
- Poor stakeholder communication.
- Uncontrolled scope changes.

## Interview Focus

Be prepared to explain how you gather requirements, handle changing priorities, resolve conflicting stakeholder expectations, and convert business problems into implementable engineering work.
