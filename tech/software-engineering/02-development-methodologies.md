

# Development Methodologies

## Overview

A software development methodology defines how engineering teams plan, execute, deliver, and improve software. While the Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC) describes *what* stages software goes through, development methodologies define *how* those stages are executed.

Experienced engineers should understand the strengths, weaknesses, and trade-offs of each methodology rather than treating one approach as universally correct.

## Definition

A development methodology is a structured approach that guides how software is planned, built, tested, delivered, and maintained throughout the SDLC.

## Objectives

- Improve collaboration.
- Deliver value predictably.
- Reduce project risk.
- Adapt to changing business needs.
- Improve product quality.

## Common Methodologies

| Methodology | Best For | Key Characteristics |
|-------------|----------|---------------------|
| Waterfall | Stable requirements | Sequential execution, extensive planning |
| Iterative | Incremental improvement | Frequent refinement based on feedback |
| Agile | Evolving products | Short iterations, customer collaboration |
| Scrum | Product development | Sprint-based Agile framework |
| Kanban | Continuous delivery | Flow optimization and work-in-progress limits |
| DevOps | Production engineering | Development and operations working together |

## Engineering Trade-offs

| Methodology | Advantages | Limitations |
|-------------|------------|-------------|
| Waterfall | Predictable planning | Poor adaptability |
| Agile | Fast feedback | Requires disciplined teams |
| Scrum | Clear cadence | Ceremony overhead |
| Kanban | Continuous flow | Less predictable scheduling |
| DevOps | Faster, reliable releases | Significant automation investment |

## Modern Perspective

Modern engineering organizations rarely follow a single methodology. Successful teams combine Agile planning, DevOps automation, continuous testing, observability, and AI-assisted development into an adaptive engineering workflow.

## Best Practices

- Choose methodology based on business context.
- Optimize for customer feedback.
- Automate wherever practical.
- Measure delivery outcomes rather than process compliance.
- Continuously improve engineering practices.

## Common Mistakes

- Treating Agile as the goal instead of delivering value.
- Blindly following ceremonies.
- Choosing methodologies because they are popular.
- Ignoring engineering metrics.
- Separating development from operations.

## Interview Focus

Be prepared to explain why modern organizations combine Agile, DevOps, CI/CD, observability, and AI-assisted development instead of following a single methodology.
