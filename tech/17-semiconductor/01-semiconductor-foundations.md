## What Is a Semiconductor?

A semiconductor is a material whose electrical conductivity lies between that of a conductor and an insulator. Its conductivity can be controlled and manipulated, making it ideal for building electronic components.

Examples include:

- Silicon (Si)
- Germanium (Ge)
- Gallium Nitride (GaN)
- Silicon Carbide (SiC)
- Gallium Arsenide (GaAs)

Silicon remains the most widely used semiconductor material because of its:

- Availability
- Manufacturing maturity
- Cost effectiveness
- Reliability
- Scalability

---

## Why Silicon Changed Computing

Silicon made modern computing possible because it enabled engineers to manufacture:

- Transistors
- Integrated Circuits
- Microprocessors
- Memory Chips
- Graphics Processors
- AI Accelerators

Modern processors contain billions of transistors.

Examples:

| Processor | Approximate Transistor Count |
|----------|-----------------------------|
| Intel 4004 | ~2,300 |
| Intel Pentium | ~3 Million |
| Modern CPUs | Tens of Billions |
| Modern AI Accelerators | Hundreds of Billions |

Semiconductor innovation continues to drive improvements in:

- Performance
- Efficiency
- Power Consumption
- Computing Density
- AI Capabilities
