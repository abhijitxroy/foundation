# Incident Response & Compliance

## Overview

No organization can completely eliminate security incidents. Despite preventive controls, systems may still experience cyberattacks, data breaches, insider threats, or operational failures. Effective Incident Response enables organizations to detect, contain, eradicate, recover from, and learn from security incidents while minimizing business impact.

Compliance complements incident response by ensuring that systems, processes, and security controls satisfy legal, regulatory, contractual, and organizational requirements.

## Definition

**Incident Response** is the engineering discipline of preparing for, detecting, analyzing, containing, eradicating, recovering from, and learning from security incidents.

**Compliance** is the process of ensuring that systems and operations adhere to applicable laws, regulations, standards, and organizational security policies.

Together, they strengthen organizational resilience, reduce operational risk, and support business continuity.

## Objectives

- Detect security incidents quickly.
- Minimize operational impact.
- Recover systems safely.
- Preserve forensic evidence.
- Meet regulatory obligations.
- Continuously improve security posture.

## Core Concepts

| Concept | Purpose |
|---------|---------|
| Incident | Security event requiring investigation and response. |
| Alert | Notification of suspicious activity. |
| Detection | Identify abnormal or malicious behavior. |
| Containment | Limit the spread and impact of an incident. |
| Eradication | Remove the root cause of the incident. |
| Recovery | Restore systems to normal operation. |
| Post-Incident Review | Identify lessons and improve future responses. |

## Incident Categories

| Category | Example |
|----------|---------|
| Malware | Ransomware, trojans, spyware |
| Unauthorized Access | Account compromise, privilege escalation |
| Data Breach | Exposure of confidential information |
| Denial of Service | Service disruption through excessive traffic |
| Insider Threat | Malicious or accidental actions by trusted users |
| Supply Chain Attack | Compromise through third-party software or services |

## Compliance Frameworks

| Framework | Purpose |
|-----------|---------|
| ISO/IEC 27001 | Information Security Management System (ISMS) |
| NIST Cybersecurity Framework | Risk-based cybersecurity guidance |
| SOC 2 | Security controls for service organizations |
| PCI DSS | Protect payment card information |
| GDPR | Protect personal data and privacy |
| HIPAA | Protect healthcare information (United States) |

Compliance requirements vary by industry, geography, and organizational obligations.

## Business Continuity vs Disaster Recovery

| Business Continuity | Disaster Recovery |
|---------------------|-------------------|
| Maintain critical business operations | Restore systems and infrastructure after disruption |
| Focus on organizational resilience | Focus on technical recovery |
| Covers people, processes, and technology | Primarily covers IT systems and data |

## Best Practices

- Maintain a documented incident response plan.
- Define clear incident severity levels and escalation paths.
- Collect and preserve forensic evidence.
- Regularly conduct incident response exercises.
- Continuously monitor security events.
- Review incidents and improve security controls.

## Common Mistakes

- Delayed incident reporting.
- Poor communication during incidents.
- Failing to preserve evidence.
- Missing post-incident reviews.
- Treating compliance as a one-time audit.
- Ignoring lessons learned.
