# Gas Turbines

A study roadmap for gas-turbine engines and power systems that produce mechanical power or thrust through the continuous flow of air and hot gases.

## Core Topics

| Topic | What to Understand |
|------|---------------------|
| Gas Turbine Basics | How air enters, is compressed, mixed with fuel, burned, and expanded through a turbine or nozzle. |
| Turbojets | Basic jet engines that generate thrust primarily from high-speed exhaust gases. |
| Turbofans | Turbine engines using a fan to produce a large portion of total thrust. |
| Turboprops | Turbine engines that use most of their available power to drive a propeller. |
| Turboshafts | Gas turbines designed primarily to produce shaft power rather than direct jet thrust. |
| Industrial Gas Turbines | Gas turbines used for electricity generation, mechanical drives, and industrial applications. |

## Study Progression

1. Understand the basic gas-turbine cycle.
2. Learn the Brayton cycle.
3. Study compressors.
4. Study combustion chambers.
5. Study turbines.
6. Understand turbojet engines.
7. Study turbofan engines.
8. Study turboprop engines.
9. Study turboshaft engines.
10. Understand industrial gas turbines.
11. Compare performance and efficiency.
12. Explore advanced gas-turbine technologies.

## Long-Term Goal

Be able to look at a gas-turbine engine and explain:

- How air flows through the engine.
- How compression occurs.
- How fuel is burned.
- How the turbine extracts energy.
- How thrust or shaft power is produced.
- Why different gas-turbine configurations exist.
- Why a turbofan is suitable for some aircraft while a turboshaft is suitable for a helicopter.
- How gas turbines differ from piston engines, electric motors, steam turbines, and rocket engines.
