# Engines

A lifelong learning roadmap for understanding engines, motors, power systems, and propulsion technologies used across land, water, air, space, industry, and emerging technologies.

The term **engine** is used broadly here. The scope includes systems that convert energy into mechanical power, electrical power, or thrust, including engines, motors, turbines, reactors, and propulsion systems.

## Learning Areas

| Area | Description |
|------|-------------|
| Fundamentals | Energy, power, torque, thrust, efficiency, thermodynamics, combustion, heat transfer, and propulsion fundamentals. |
| Internal Combustion | Petrol, diesel, two-stroke, four-stroke, rotary, opposed-piston, direct-injection, turbocharged, and supercharged engines. |
| External Combustion | Steam engines, steam turbines, Stirling engines, and other external-combustion systems. |
| Gas Turbines | Turbojets, turbofans, turboprops, turboshafts, and industrial gas turbines. |
| Electric Motors | DC, AC, induction, permanent-magnet, brushless motors, and motor controllers. |
| Hybrid & Alternative Power | Hybrid systems, plug-in hybrids, hydrogen engines, fuel cells, battery-electric systems, and alternative fuels. |
| Aircraft Propulsion | Piston, turboprop, turbofan, turbojet, turboshaft, ramjet, scramjet, pulsejet, and electric aircraft propulsion. |
| Marine Propulsion | Marine diesel, gas turbine, steam turbine, diesel-electric, nuclear, electric, hybrid, and alternative-fuel propulsion. |
| Rocket Propulsion | Solid, liquid, hybrid, monopropellant, bipropellant, and cryogenic rocket engines. |
| Space Propulsion | Chemical, ion, Hall-effect, gridded-ion, electrothermal, electrospray, nuclear-electric, nuclear-thermal, solar-electric, cold-gas, and solar-sail propulsion. |
| Nuclear Power & Propulsion | Nuclear reactors, nuclear steam turbines, nuclear marine propulsion, nuclear thermal propulsion, and nuclear electric propulsion. |
| Industrial & Power | Generator engines, industrial gas turbines, steam turbines, microturbines, combined-cycle power, agricultural engines, and construction equipment engines. |
| Vehicle Applications | Cars, motorcycles, scooters, trucks, lorries, buses, trains, tanks, ships, boats, submarines, aircraft, helicopters, drones, spacecraft, and satellites. |
| Emerging Propulsion | Hydrogen, fusion, antimatter, beamed-energy, solar-sail, and other advanced propulsion concepts. |

## Study Approach

The learning path should progress from fundamentals to increasingly specialized propulsion technologies:

1. Understand energy, power, torque, thrust, efficiency, and thermodynamics.
2. Learn the basic principles of mechanical and thermal engines.
3. Understand internal-combustion and external-combustion engines.
4. Study gas turbines and electric motors.
5. Understand hybrid, alternative-fuel, and fuel-cell systems.
6. Explore aircraft and marine propulsion.
7. Study rocket engines and space propulsion.
8. Understand nuclear power and propulsion.
9. Explore industrial and power-generation systems.
10. Compare how different propulsion technologies are applied to different vehicles.
11. Explore emerging and experimental propulsion technologies.

## Long-Term Goal

Build a broad and practical understanding of how different machines generate power and thrust, why different propulsion systems are chosen, how they work, their advantages and limitations, and where they are used.

This is a **wishlist**, not a completed knowledge base. Topics will be studied and documented progressively over time.
