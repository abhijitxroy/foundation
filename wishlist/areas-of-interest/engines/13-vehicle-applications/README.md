# Vehicle Applications

A study roadmap for understanding how different engines, motors, turbines, and propulsion systems are selected and integrated into different types of vehicles.

This section focuses on the **application side** of propulsion: what powers a vehicle, how that power reaches the wheels, propeller, rotor, track, or other propulsion mechanism, and why a particular system is suitable for the vehicle.

## Core Applications

| Vehicle | What to Understand |
|---------|---------------------|
| Cars | Petrol, diesel, hybrid, battery-electric, hydrogen, and other automotive powertrains. |
| Motorcycles | Petrol, electric, hybrid, and performance-oriented motorcycle power systems. |
| Scooters | Small petrol engines, electric motors, batteries, and urban electric powertrains. |
| Trucks and Lorries | Heavy-duty diesel, CNG, LNG, battery-electric, hydrogen, and hybrid powertrains. |
| Buses | Diesel, CNG, LNG, hybrid, battery-electric, and hydrogen fuel-cell propulsion. |
| Trains | Diesel-mechanical, diesel-electric, electric, battery-electric, hydrogen, and hybrid propulsion. |
| Tanks | High-power diesel and gas-turbine propulsion, transmissions, and auxiliary power systems. |
| Ships | Large marine diesel, gas turbine, steam turbine, diesel-electric, nuclear, electric, and hybrid propulsion. |
| Boats | Petrol, diesel, electric, hybrid, and waterjet propulsion systems. |
| Submarines | Diesel-electric, air-independent, battery-electric, and nuclear propulsion systems. |
| Aircraft | Piston, turboprop, turbofan, turbojet, and emerging electric or hybrid propulsion. |
| Helicopters | Turboshaft, piston, and emerging electric or hybrid propulsion systems. |
| Drones | Electric motors, batteries, hybrid systems, fuel cells, and specialized propulsion. |
| Spacecraft | Chemical, electric, nuclear, and other spacecraft propulsion systems. |
| Satellites | Chemical thrusters, electric propulsion, cold-gas systems, and other station-keeping technologies. |
