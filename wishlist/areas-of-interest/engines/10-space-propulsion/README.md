# Space Propulsion

A study roadmap for propulsion systems used to move, orient, and maneuver spacecraft after they are in space.

Unlike launch propulsion, which is primarily concerned with producing high thrust to overcome Earth's gravity and atmospheric drag, space propulsion often emphasizes efficient use of propellant, precise control, long-duration operation, and high total delta-v.

## Core Topics

| Topic | What to Understand |
|------|---------------------|
| Chemical Propulsion | Chemical thrusters used for spacecraft maneuvering, orbit changes, attitude control, and other high-thrust applications. |
| Ion Thrusters | Electric propulsion systems that accelerate ions using electric fields to produce very high exhaust velocity. |
| Hall-Effect Thrusters | Plasma propulsion systems that use electric and magnetic fields to accelerate ions. |
| Gridded Ion Thrusters | Electric propulsion systems using grids to accelerate ions electrostatically. |
| Electrothermal Propulsion | Systems that electrically heat a propellant before expanding it through a nozzle. |
| Electrospray Propulsion | Micropropulsion systems that electrically accelerate charged droplets or ions. |
| Nuclear-Electric Propulsion | Nuclear power used to generate electricity for electric propulsion systems. |
| Nuclear-Thermal Propulsion | Nuclear heat used to heat a propellant and produce high-velocity exhaust. |
| Solar-Electric Propulsion | Solar-generated electricity used to operate electric propulsion systems. |
| Cold-Gas Propulsion | Stored gas expanded through a nozzle to generate thrust without combustion. |
| Solar Sails | Propulsion using radiation pressure from sunlight rather than conventional propellant. |

## Electric Propulsion

Understand the basic principle:

1. Generate or store electrical power.
2. Ionize a propellant.
3. Accelerate charged particles.
4. Produce high exhaust velocity.
5. Generate continuous low thrust over long periods.
