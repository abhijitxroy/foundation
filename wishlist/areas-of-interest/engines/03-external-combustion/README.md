# External Combustion Engines

A study roadmap for systems where heat is supplied to a working fluid from outside the engine or expansion machine. This area covers historical and modern systems that convert external heat into mechanical power.

## Core Topics

| Topic | What to Understand |
|------|---------------------|
| Steam Engines | How external heat produces steam and how expanding steam produces mechanical work. |
| Steam Turbines | How high-pressure steam expands through turbine stages to produce rotational power. |
| Stirling Engines | Closed-cycle external-combustion engines that use temperature differences to produce mechanical work. |
| Other External Combustion | Other systems that use an external heat source to drive a working fluid or power-conversion system. |

## Study Progression

1. Understand the concept of external combustion.
2. Learn how steam engines work.
3. Understand the Rankine cycle.
4. Study steam turbines.
5. Understand turbine stages and expansion.
6. Learn how condensers and feedwater systems work.
7. Study Stirling engines.
8. Compare steam engines, steam turbines, and Stirling engines.
9. Understand efficiency and heat losses.
10. Explore modern applications and specialized systems.

## Long-Term Goal

Be able to explain:

- How an external heat source becomes mechanical power.
- How a steam engine differs from a steam turbine.
- How a Stirling engine works.
- How the Rankine and Stirling cycles operate.
- Why steam turbines dominate large-scale steam power generation.
- How nuclear and other heat sources can drive external power-conversion systems.
- The advantages and limitations of external-combustion systems compared with internal-combustion engines and other propulsion technologies.
