# Internal Combustion Engines

A study roadmap for engines that generate mechanical power by burning fuel inside the engine.

## Core Topics

| Topic | What to Understand |
|------|---------------------|
| Four-Stroke Engines | Intake, compression, power, and exhaust strokes and how the cycle produces power. |
| Two-Stroke Engines | Two-stroke operating cycle, power delivery, scavenging, lubrication, and applications. |
| Petrol Engines | Spark ignition, air-fuel mixture, combustion, performance, and applications. |
| Diesel Engines | Compression ignition, fuel injection, combustion, efficiency, and applications. |
| Rotary Engines | Rotary combustion principles, components, advantages, limitations, and applications. |
| Opposed-Piston Engines | Two pistons sharing a cylinder, combustion arrangement, efficiency, and applications. |
| Direct Injection | Direct fuel injection, injection timing, atomization, combustion, and efficiency. |
| Turbocharging | Exhaust-driven compressor systems, boost pressure, intercooling, and performance. |
| Supercharging | Mechanically driven compressors, boost generation, advantages, and limitations. |

## Study Progression

1. Understand how a basic internal-combustion engine works.
2. Learn the four-stroke cycle.
3. Learn the two-stroke cycle.
4. Compare petrol and diesel engines.
5. Understand engine components and configurations.
6. Study combustion and fuel systems.
7. Learn direct injection.
8. Study turbocharging and supercharging.
9. Understand engine performance and efficiency.
10. Compare different engine designs and applications.
11. Explore advanced and alternative internal-combustion technologies.

## Long-Term Goal

Be able to look at any internal-combustion engine and understand:

- What fuel it uses.
- How combustion occurs.
- What thermodynamic cycle it follows.
- How power is produced.
- How the engine is configured.
- How air and fuel enter the engine.
- How exhaust gases leave the engine.
- How the engine is cooled and lubricated.
- How power and torque are controlled.
- Why that particular engine design is suitable for its application.
