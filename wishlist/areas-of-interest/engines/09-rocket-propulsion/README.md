# Rocket Propulsion

A study roadmap for propulsion systems that generate thrust by accelerating and expelling propellant. This area focuses on rocket engines and the technologies used to launch and maneuver vehicles in space.

## Core Topics

| Topic | What to Understand |
|------|---------------------|
| Rocket Engine Basics | How rockets generate thrust without depending on atmospheric oxygen. |
| Solid Rocket Motors | Solid propellant, motor casing, grain geometry, ignition, combustion, and nozzle operation. |
| Liquid Rocket Engines | Liquid propellants, tanks, pumps, injectors, combustion chambers, and nozzles. |
| Hybrid Rocket Engines | Solid fuel combined with a separate liquid or gaseous oxidizer. |
| Monopropellant Engines | Single propellant decomposed or otherwise converted into hot gas to generate thrust. |
| Bipropellant Engines | Separate fuel and oxidizer combined in a combustion chamber. |
| Cryogenic Engines | Rocket engines using very low-temperature propellants such as liquid oxygen and liquid hydrogen or methane. |

5. Study liquid rocket engines.
6. Learn hybrid rocket engines.
7. Understand monopropellant and bipropellant systems.
8. Study rocket nozzles.
9. Learn major engine cycles.
10. Study cryogenic propulsion.
11. Understand staging and delta-v.

13. Compare rocket engines across different missions.
14. Explore advanced rocket technologies.

## Long-Term Goal

Be able to look at any rocket engine and understand:

- What propellants it uses.
- How the propellants are stored and fed.
- How combustion or another energy-conversion process generates hot gas.
- How the combustion chamber and nozzle generate thrust.
- Which engine cycle it uses.
- Why that cycle was selected.
- What determines its specific impulse and thrust.
- How the engine fits into the complete launch vehicle or spacecraft propulsion system.
- What trade-offs exist between performance, mass, complexity, reliability, cost, and reusability.
